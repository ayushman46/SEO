# Pricing research 2: buyers' own words, demand signals, the exact gap (2026-11-23)

Scope: market-gap and willingness-to-pay evidence for noticr. This file does not repeat `MARKET_RESEARCH.md`, `MARKET_RESEARCH_2_PRODUCT.md`, `MARKET_RESEARCH_3_FULL.md` or `CONVERSION_RESEARCH.md`; where an earlier finding is needed it is marked "(earlier doc)".

## How to read this

- Every page listed was opened on 2026-11-23 unless marked otherwise. The date next to a quote is the date shown on the page.
- Pages were read through an automated page reader. Quotes are as the reader returned them; **re-open the page and check the wording before reusing any quote publicly**.
- Labels: **[B]** buyer speaking first-hand. **[V]** vendor or seller source. **[R]** relayed by a third party. **[S]** seen only in a search-result snippet, page not opened: treat as a lead, not evidence.
- **Not accessible** (HTTP 403 or equivalent, not worked around): reddit.com, indiehackers.com, community.getjobber.com, biggerpockets.com, flyingsolo.com.au, ukbusinessforums.co.uk, blackhatworld.com, consumeraffairs.com, bbb.org, g2.com, fiverr.com, upwork.com, searchengineland.com (Backlinko survey page), emarketer.com, skynova.com, therodinhoods.com. - Selection bias, stated once: most first-hand quotes come from complaint reviews of large small-business marketing vendors on Trustpilot. They show what angry buyers paid, not what satisfied buyers paid or what anyone would pay noticr.

### 1.2 The one survey of buyers found (old)

Backlinko with Northstar Research Partners, online survey of 1,200 US small business owners and decision makers, 28 March to 1 April 2019 (press release 2019-07-02) [V, research partner named]: average spend $497/month on SEO services; 65% have worked with several providers and 25% with three or more; reasons for leaving: 44% dissatisfaction with business results, 34% poor customer service; only 30% would recommend their current provider; found via referrals 28%, Google 26%, review sites 18%; clients spending over $500/month were 53% more likely to be satisfied. https://www.newswire.com/news/american-small-businesses-spend-497-per-month-on-seo-services-new-20931082 (also https://www.searchenginejournal.com/clients-who-pay-more-for-seo-services-report-higher-satisfaction-rates/315067). Seven years old; no newer buyer-side survey with amounts was found.

BrightLocal, 1,002 US local business owners with 1-50 staff, 2026-10-21 [V]: 85% take primary responsibility for marketing decisions; barriers to improving online visibility: too technical 28%, already enough customers 26%, not convinced it makes a difference 25%, past efforts showed no results 23%, lack of time 23%, lack of budget 19%. No spend figures. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/smb-report-who-owns-marketing/

### 1.4 Reasons for distrust, in order of how often they appear in the opened posts

1. **Nothing visible was delivered** (rows 1, 2, 7, 8, 11, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21). The buyer cannot point to any change.
2. **Could not verify the work** (row 19: "I had no way to verify"; Juel: only the home page was done).
3. **Lock-in and exit penalties** (rows 9, 10, 13) and **billing after cancellation** (rows 5, 14).
4. **The vendor holds the asset**: site lost on leaving (Matt, Hibu), domain "hijacked" (row 16), phone number replaced by a tracking number (row 10).
5. **Price creep after a low entry price** (row 19: $160 became $545; row 8: £2k then £4.5k more).
6. **Promised numbers not met** (row 9: "I was promised 253 guaranteed leads over 12 months").
7. **Cold outreach and anonymity** (jct1111; Michael Hart).
8. **Scope vagueness** (row 13; Juel).

### 1.5 What would make them buy a one-time fix

Direct statements ("I would pay $X for a one-time fix") were **not found** on any page that could be opened. What the opened posts support, indirectly:

- A buyer explicitly asking for "Fixed price only" (mshook3, 2026-07-12).
- A buyer accepting $160 because it looked low-risk, then leaving when the price climbed and the work could not be verified (row 19). Reading: a low fixed price gets the first purchase; unverifiable work and add-ons lose the customer.
- One client describing "technical sprint first, then in-house" as what worked (michaelkong).
- A PeoplePerHour seller puts "No Monthly Lock-In" in an offer title (section 2.2), which shows sellers believe lock-in is the objection.

No first-hand quote from an Indian buyer stating a rupee amount was found, and only three indie-founder quotes with amounts (rows 22-24).

### The single sharpest gap

Price is not the gap: one-off SEO fixes already sell for $50 to $300, and owners are already paying $240 to $840 a month for bundles that they say deliver nothing they can see. The gap is a **no-contract, fixed-price fix of the exact problems a free audit has already shown the owner, with before/after proof for every item, a named person, and nothing the owner loses by leaving**, on the site builders small owners actually use. The AI-specific work (wrong facts, crawler access, facts in raw HTML) is what makes that offer different from a marketplace gig, but on its own it has no visible buyers yet, so it should ride inside the fix, not lead it.

### The first 100 customers

- **Role:** the owner, who makes the marketing decision alone (85% of local owners do, BrightLocal 2026) and has no marketing employee. Not a marketer.
- **Business type:** (a) single-location local service businesses with a site on Wix, Squarespace, WordPress or a vendor-built site: trades, cleaning, clinics, spas, lenders, builders (the trades named in the Thryv, Yell, Hibu and BrandRep reviews); (b) small Shopify store owners who have already paid $160 to $700 to a one-off "expert"; (c) a smaller third group, bootstrapped founders who cancelled a $2,000-a-month agency. Group (c) leans to do-it-yourself and is the least likely to buy a service.
- **Trigger that makes them look:** they have just cancelled, or are trying to leave, a monthly marketing or SEO contract after several months with no leads and no visible work; second, an unexplained traffic drop (often after a core update) or a drop after a rebrand or migration; third, they asked ChatGPT about their category and saw only competitors.
- **Where they are found online:** searching for how to cancel or replace Thryv, Hibu, Yell and similar bundles, and reading those vendors' Trustpilot pages; Shopify Community SEO threads ("is this expert a scam", "traffic dropped", "fixed price only"); the Alignable owner forum; the Wix and Squarespace app and help ecosystems; PeoplePerHour, where they already buy $10 to $390 audits. Consultants who advise them sit in Local Search Forum (a route to white-label, not to owners). Reddit, Indie Hackers and the Jobber community are likely venues but could not be opened. In India: buyers of ₹5,000 to ₹15,000 a month SEO on IndiaMART [S, not verified].
- **What the first purchase looks like:** $150 to $300 one-time, card payment without a call, scope listed item by item, proof delivered with the work.

Caution: do not contact reviewers directly; publish for the searches they make.

| | No controlled test of rupee pricing for a service. | **Not found** | |

What the evidence supports for noticr's price page, without overreach:

1. Show every price, including a "from" price for the sprint; the one test found favours a low visible starting price over no price, and a high visible starting price can hurt.
2. Keep the paid entry small but fix its scope in writing, and never raise the price mid-job; the only first-hand story of a low entry price shows escalation destroying the sale.
3. Lead with one-time; keep the $29 monitoring as an option, with cancellation in one click and no minimum term, because lock-in is the thing these buyers are escaping.
4. Guarantee delivery, not outcomes: each listed fix applied and shown before/after, or that item refunded.
5. A split payment (part up front, the rest on delivery with proof) matches what freelancers already offer these buyers, but there is no data that it converts better.
6. Rupee prices should be set against the ₹5,000 to ₹8,000 monthly anchor and tested; nothing published tells you the right number.
